# Masters' Hall - Canon-Constrained Reconstruction Brief

## 1. What is explicitly known
- Masters' Hall is a grey-stone University building associated with the Masters.
- It shows stained glass at night.
- It contains living quarters for the Masters, resident gillers, and guest rooms for visiting arcanists.
- It includes a large windowless room with a familiar crescent-shaped table.
- That formal chamber is distinct from the raised admissions theatre in Hollows.

## 2. What is strongly implied
- The building is mixed residential and administrative rather than purely ceremonial.
- The crescent-table room is a governance or deliberation chamber.
- Masters' Hall is tied to the Hollows side of campus by known travel descriptions.

## 3. What is weakly inferred
- Private offices may exist, but they are not the only or best-locked function.
- The building likely has separated private residential rooms and a more formal shared chamber.
- Guest accommodation is integrated into the overall hall rather than detached elsewhere.

## 4. What must not be contradicted
- Do not turn the whole building into nothing but offices.
- Do not erase the residential role.
- Do not merge the crescent-table chamber with the admissions theatre.
- Do not depict the major formal chamber as windowed if using the known windowless description.

## 5. Likely building function and circulation
Masters' Hall likely serves as the residential and governance center for the senior academic hierarchy, combining private quarters, guest rooms, and at least one shared formal chamber. Circulation probably separates private lodging from the more formal meeting space.

## 6. Likely entrances, stairs, and room zones
- Main entrance or approach from the Hollows side of campus.
- Private residential rooms.
- Guest rooms.
- Service circulation for a lived-in hall.
- One formal windowless meeting chamber with crescent table.

## 7. Unknowns that must remain unknown
- Exact office count.
- Exact floor count.
- Exact relation of quarters to meeting chamber.
- Exact exterior massing beyond grey stone and stained glass presence.

## 8. Final reconstruction summary
Masters' Hall should be reconstructed as a mixed residential-governance building for the University's senior hierarchy, not as a pure office block. It needs private living quarters, guest accommodation, and a separate formal crescent-table chamber, with enough architectural dignity to distinguish it from ordinary academic rooms.
