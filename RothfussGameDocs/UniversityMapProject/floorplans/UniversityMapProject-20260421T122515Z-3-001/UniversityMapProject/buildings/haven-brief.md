# Haven - Building Brief

## Building Identity
The University's northern asylum-compound: formal, beautiful at first glance, and deeply controlled beneath the polish.

## Locked Canon
- Reached outside the dense campus core by road from the north side.
- Huge, bigger than the Artificery, and more like a manor or institutional estate than a prison or dungeon.
- Red tile roof, high windows, arched doorways, pillars, fountains, flowers, hedges, and formal grounds.
- Enclosed by a nearly ten-foot wrought-iron fence that is effectively unclimbable.
- Main entry is grand, with stained glass and vaulted ceilings.
- Interior includes a wide marble staircase and long white hallways lined with wooden doors.
- Supports roughly 320 to 350 people with room for around 150 more.
- Patient rooms vary by safety and containment level.
- Alder Whin's room is daylit but padded across walls, ceiling, and floor for sound suppression and safety.
- Elodin's former cell is in a different wing and uses a solid copper door, bare grey stone, unusual pressure in the air, huge copper-framed windows, no inside handle, and anti-escape logic.

## Frozen Design Decisions
- Treat Haven as a walled estate-compound with multiple wings rather than one blunt rectangular ward.
- Keep the public face grand and almost reassuring, then let the secure logic reveal itself deeper inside.
- Separate ordinary patient housing, padded safety rooms, and high-containment cells into different circulation zones or wings.

## Major Zones
- Perimeter fence and guarded gate logic.
- Formal arrival court and entrance hall.
- Vertical core with marble stair.
- Long ward corridors.
- Standard patient rooms.
- Specialized safety rooms.
- Segregated high-containment wing or chamber set.

## Hard Bans
- No Masters' offices.
- No use as the normal administrative center of the University.
- No dungeon-block exterior.
- No unfenced open campus siting.
- No tiny-scale interpretation.

## Prompt-Ready Summary
Large manor-like mental institution north of the main campus, wrapped in formal gardens and an unclimbable wrought-iron fence. The approach should feel almost stately, with stained glass, vaulted entry, and marble stair, but the deeper plan must support a huge patient population and increasingly specialized containment. Use separate wings or corridor systems for ordinary care, padded safety rooms, and extreme copper-based high containment.
