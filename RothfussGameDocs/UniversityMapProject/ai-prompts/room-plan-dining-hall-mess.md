Create a strictly top-down room plan for the main dining hall in the Mess from Patrick Rothfuss's *Kingkiller Chronicle* as a canon-constrained reconstruction.

## ROOM IDENTITY
This is a large communal eating hall for a substantial student body, not a tavern room or ceremonial feast chamber.

## LOCKED CANON
- The Mess dining room can seat roughly two hundred students.
- It uses large plank tables.

## STRONG IMPLICATIONS
- The hall needs broad circulation aisles for serving and crowd movement.
- One side or end should connect toward kitchen or serving space.
- The room should read as practical, worn, and institutional.

## ROOM ENVELOPE AND PLACEMENT DISCIPLINE
- Use a broad rectangular or slightly elongated hall.
- Put the main public door on the side that fits the lawn/Mews approach.
- Add one service connection to kitchen or pantry, not many random doors.
- If windows are used, place them only along true exterior walls and keep them evenly practical.
- Keep the room clear enough that the table layout remains legible at once.

## FIXED ELEMENTS AND FURNITURE
- Fill the room with rows of large plank tables and benches.
- Keep a dominant central aisle or a pair of main aisles.
- Use a serving edge, hatch, or sideboard only near the kitchen connection.
- Avoid decorative furniture unrelated to dining service.

## LIGHT AND MATERIALS
- Materials should feel hard-wearing: plank tops, benches, stone or timber walls, plain floor.
- Light should be practical rather than theatrical.

## HARD BANS
- no tiny common room scale
- no fancy noble banquet layout
- no chairs-at-each-place modern dining look
- no missing service connection

## LABELS TO INCLUDE
- `main entry`
- `plank tables`
- `benches`
- `service door`
- `kitchen side`

## UNCERTAINTY HANDLING
If the exact number of tables is uncertain:
- preserve the approximate large capacity
- prefer fewer long tables over many tiny ones
- keep the hall open enough to breathe

## CONSISTENCY REQUIREMENTS
- Keep north aligned with the master campus map.
- Keep scale consistent with the approved building shell.
- Preserve previously approved entrances, corridors, stairs, and adjacent rooms.
- Do not contradict earlier approved plans.
- If a new detail would force a contradiction, reject the detail rather than improvising.
