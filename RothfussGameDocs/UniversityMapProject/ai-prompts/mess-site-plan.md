Create a strictly top-down, orthographic architectural site plan of the Mess and its immediate exterior context from Patrick Rothfuss's *Kingkiller Chronicle*.

This is a canon-constrained reconstruction, not fantasy concept art.

## OUTPUT GOAL
Produce a single black-and-white site plan showing:
- the full footprint of the Mess
- the lawn relationship to the Mews to the north
- the long, low-roofed character of the building
- only enough surrounding context to explain the main dining approach and the likely service side

## LOCKED CANON
- The Mess is a long, low-roofed building.
- It lies across the lawn from the Mews.
- Its dining hall can seat roughly two hundred students at large plank tables.

## STRONG IMPLICATIONS
- The Mess is dominated by one very large communal hall.
- The building needs a clear public approach from the lawn and Mews side.
- A kitchen or service edge is strongly implied, but exact configuration remains reconstructed.

## OPENING AND MASSING DISCIPLINE
- Keep the footprint elongated rather than compact or radial.
- Let the long side facing the lawn read as the social approach face.
- Use one main public entrance on the lawn-facing side unless a second small service door is clearly labeled reconstructed.
- Exterior windows may repeat along the long walls, but keep them practical rather than ceremonial.
- Keep the roofline low and functional; do not turn the Mess into a great cathedral refectory.

## HARD BANS
- no tall ceremonial hall silhouette
- no separation from the Mews lawn relationship
- no many-wing complex without evidence
- no non-dining identity

## LABELS TO INCLUDE
- `the Mess`
- `lawn to Mews`
- `main entrance`
- `service side` if used

## UNCERTAINTY HANDLING
If kitchen or pantry volumes are shown externally:
- keep them modest
- place them on the rear or side away from the main lawn approach
- label them reconstructed if needed

## CONSISTENCY REQUIREMENTS
- Keep north aligned with the master campus map.
- Keep scale consistent with the approved building shell.
- Preserve previously approved entrances, corridors, stairs, and adjacent rooms.
- Do not contradict earlier approved plans.
- If a new detail would force a contradiction, reject the detail rather than improvising.
