Create a strictly top-down room plan for the acquisitions office in the Archives from Patrick Rothfuss's *Kingkiller Chronicle* as a canon-constrained reconstruction.

## ROOM IDENTITY
This is a tiny, perpetually dark staff office inside the Archives, dominated by one enormous operational wall map.

## LOCKED CANON
- The acquisitions office is tiny.
- It is perpetually dark.
- One whole wall is dominated by a huge lacquered map.
- The map tracks cities, roads, book rumors, and acquisition teams.

## STRONG IMPLICATIONS
- There should be very little spare floor area.
- The map wall is the main architectural and visual anchor.
- Desk space, records, and working notes belong near the map.

## ROOM ENVELOPE AND PLACEMENT DISCIPLINE
- Use one simple staff door into a cramped room.
- Dedicate one full wall to the large map and keep it visually dominant.
- Place the desk or main work surface so the map can be consulted directly from it.
- Shelves, cabinets, or pigeonholes may occupy the remaining wall space conservatively.
- Avoid windows unless you can justify a real exterior wall and still preserve the room's perpetual darkness; in most cases, no window is safer.

## FIXED ELEMENTS AND FURNITURE
- The wall map is mandatory and should dwarf other furnishings.
- Include one desk or table, one or two chairs or stools, and compact storage.
- Keep circulation tight.

## LIGHT AND MATERIALS
- The room should feel dim and staff-only.
- Materials should be lacquered map surface, wood desk, shelves, and the Archives' austere interior fabric.

## HARD BANS
- no spacious office suite
- no bright sunlit room
- no second giant feature that competes with the map wall
- no decorative library-reading-room layout

## LABELS TO INCLUDE
- `door`
- `map wall`
- `desk`
- `records`

## UNCERTAINTY HANDLING
If extra storage is uncertain:
- keep it compact
- preserve the tiny-room feeling
- do not turn the office into a larger archives department floor

## CONSISTENCY REQUIREMENTS
- Keep north aligned with the master campus map.
- Keep scale consistent with the approved building shell.
- Preserve previously approved entrances, corridors, stairs, and adjacent rooms.
- Do not contradict earlier approved plans.
- If a new detail would force a contradiction, reject the detail rather than improvising.
