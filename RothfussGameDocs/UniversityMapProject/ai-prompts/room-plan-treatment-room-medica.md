Create a strictly top-down room plan for a treatment room in the Medica from Patrick Rothfuss's *Kingkiller Chronicle* as a canon-constrained reconstruction.

## ROOM IDENTITY
This room should read as a practical University treatment room inside a larger medical institution, not as a bedroom or office.

## LOCKED CANON
- The Medica is a real treatment institution, not just a student dormitory.
- It serves both University patients and townspeople.

## STRONG IMPLICATIONS
- The room should support examination, treatment, and short-term care.
- Entry is most likely from a corridor on the treatment side of the Medica.
- Modest storage and washing facilities are strongly implied.

## ROOM ENVELOPE AND PLACEMENT DISCIPLINE
- Put one main door on the corridor-facing wall.
- If a window is used, place it only on an exterior wall and keep it practical rather than decorative.
- Keep the room compact and functional, with enough clearance to move around the treatment surface.
- Avoid multiple doors unless one is clearly labeled reconstructed.

## FIXED ELEMENTS AND FURNITURE
- Include one treatment bed or examination table as the dominant element.
- Include a washstand, basin, cabinet, shelf, or small instrument table.
- One or two stools or chairs are acceptable.
- Keep furnishings sparse and work-focused rather than domestic.

## LIGHT AND MATERIALS
- Use plain institutional materials: stone or plaster walls, timber furniture, practical linens.
- Lighting should be workable and calm, not dramatic.

## HARD BANS
- no luxurious bedroom furnishing
- no giant ward-scale room
- no laboratory hot-work equipment
- no ornate fantasy healer shrine

## LABELS TO INCLUDE
- `door`
- `treatment bed`
- `washstand`
- `cabinet` or `supplies`
- `window` only if present on an exterior wall

## UNCERTAINTY HANDLING
If exact equipment is uncertain:
- keep it to a bed, washstand, cabinet, and one work surface
- do not overfill the room with invented medical devices

## CONSISTENCY REQUIREMENTS
- Keep north aligned with the master campus map.
- Keep scale consistent with the approved building shell.
- Preserve previously approved entrances, corridors, stairs, and adjacent rooms.
- Do not contradict earlier approved plans.
- If a new detail would force a contradiction, reject the detail rather than improvising.
