Create a strictly top-down floor plan for the Medica, Ground Floor, from Patrick Rothfuss's *Kingkiller Chronicle* as a canon-constrained reconstruction.

## FLOOR IDENTITY
This floor should read as a busy institutional medical level serving both University patients and townspeople, with more order than Mains but without false symmetry.

## LOCKED CANON
- The Medica is a large, oddly shaped building.
- It is taller and less rambling than Mains.
- It treats more than just students; town patients matter too.

## STRONG IMPLICATIONS
- A public receiving or waiting zone belongs near the main entrance.
- Treatment rooms, recovery space, supply storage, and staff work areas are strongly implied.
- Quieter recovery space should sit deeper or farther from the main receiving point.

## PLACEMENT DISCIPLINE
- Use an irregular but intelligible perimeter, not a neat rectangle.
- Place a main receiving entrance on the most public side of the plan.
- Put a reception or waiting area immediately inside rather than dropping patients into a ward.
- Run one clear circulation spine or bent main corridor from receiving toward treatment and recovery rooms.
- Put treatment rooms along exterior walls where conservative windows can be justified.
- Put a larger recovery ward or quieter convalescent rooms on a calmer exterior edge.
- Keep staff workroom, storage, or dispensary near the center of operations rather than at the farthest end.

## FIXED ELEMENTS AND FURNITURE
- Treatment rooms may include a treatment bed or table, cabinet, washstand, and one or two stools.
- Recovery areas may include several beds arranged with walking clearance rather than dense crowding.
- Waiting or receiving space may include benches, desks, or intake tables, but keep them functional and sparse.
- Windows are allowed only on true exterior walls and should be used conservatively.

## HARD BANS
- no tiny one-room infirmary
- no dormitory-style bedroom floor replacing treatment logic
- no perfectly rational hospital grid presented as canon
- no decorative noble-house plan

## LABELS TO INCLUDE
- `receiving`
- `treatment`
- `recovery`
- `staff`
- `supplies` or `dispensary`
- `corridor`

## UNCERTAINTY HANDLING
If a specialized ward or secondary stair is uncertain:
- keep it generic
- label reconstructed details only when necessary
- do not invent advanced departments unsupported by the books

## CONSISTENCY REQUIREMENTS
- Keep north aligned with the master campus map.
- Keep scale consistent with the approved building shell.
- Preserve previously approved entrances, corridors, stairs, and adjacent rooms.
- Do not contradict earlier approved plans.
- If a new detail would force a contradiction, reject the detail rather than improvising.
