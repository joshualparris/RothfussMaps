# The Mains - Building Brief

## Building Identity
The oldest academic heart of the University: a sprawling accreted maze that feels more grown than designed.

## Locked Canon
- Nearly an acre and a half in footprint.
- Looks cobbled together from smaller mismatched buildings.
- Has grown in all directions over time and engulfed smaller structures and courtyards.
- Internal circulation is rambling and irregular, with odd turns, dead ends, and shortcuts through workrooms and lecture halls.
- At least one courtyard is isolated enough to require climbing through a window for access.
- At least one teaching room is a descending demonstration theatre with tiered semicircular seating, raised stage area, and stone worktables.
- The roof can be reached by exterior drainpipes in a sheltered box alley.
- The rooftop itself is a familiar maze of chimneys and mixed slate, clay, and tin.
- Auri and Kvothe can descend from the roof into a courtyard by way of an apple tree.

## Frozen Design Decisions
- Treat Mains as a cluster of swallowed buildings under one institutional identity, not as one clean shell.
- Keep at least one interior courtyard truly awkward or sealed from normal circulation.
- Let roof logic mirror plan logic: irregular, layered, and navigable rather than simple.
- Do not overclaim exact entrance count or compass placement when canon only proves irregular access.

## Major Zones
- Main circulation maze: crooked halls, turnbacks, dead ends, shortcut links.
- Academic pockets: lecture theatres, workrooms, demonstration spaces.
- Absorbed voids: courtyards, trapped exterior pockets, awkwardly inherited spaces.
- Vertical oddities: stairs, roof access, upper passages, exterior climb routes.

## Hard Bans
- No symmetry.
- No single rational master plan.
- No fully regular courtyard arrangement.
- No clean modern corridor grid.
- No simple flat roof.

## Prompt-Ready Summary
Accreted multi-level stone academic labyrinth covering nearly one and a half acres, made of mismatched pieces grown together over centuries. Plan it as a building that swallowed other buildings and courtyards, with crooked circulation, hidden pockets, descending lecture rooms, exterior climb routes, and a cluttered rooftop of chimneys and mixed roofing materials. It should feel ancient, useful, and spatially inconvenient in a believable way.
