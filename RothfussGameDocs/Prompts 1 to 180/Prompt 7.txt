This refined version of Prompt 7 is architecturally superior to the initial suggestion. You have correctly identified a key "Vibe Coding" trap: **state-bloat**.
By deriving labels in the narration layer rather than storing them in the PlayerState, you are keeping the engine's "brain" purely mathematical and the narrator's "voice" purely descriptive. This is the hallmark of a maintainable simulation.
Here are my thoughts on why this specific configuration is the "Gold Standard" for your project:
### 1. The "Command Gatekeeper" Logic
Implementing a null return for non-scene commands in narrationContext.ts is a brilliant move.
 * **Efficiency:** It saves API tokens and reduces latency on frequent actions like inventory or status.
 * **Reliability:** These commands are "Interface Actions," not "Story Actions." Local deterministic rendering is always more reliable for UI than an LLM trying to be clever with a list of items.
### 2. Event-Priority Narration
The "Action-Backdrop" instruction is the fix for the most common failure in AI-driven interactive fiction: the "Static Room" problem.
 * Without this, the LLM often sees the location_summary and spends 100 words on the architecture of the Fishery, while the engine_truth.sympathy_outcome (the actual explosion the player just caused) gets relegated to a single sentence at the end.
 * **Result:** The game will feel "reactive" rather than just "descriptive."
### 3. The "Label-Only" Constraint
Removing raw numbers (hunger: 4) and replacing them with hunger_label: "your stomach is a hollow ache" forces the LLM to remain in-character.
 * Sticking to engine-supplied labels prevents the LLM from inventing its own severity levels (e.g., narrating that you are "dying of starvation" when you are only moderately hungry).
### 4. Derived Social Flavour
Deriving social_standing_label inside the narration layer based on location_id and reputation is exactly how a human DM works. They look at the stats and then decide the "vibe" of the room.
 * **Architecture Win:** If you ever change how Reputation is calculated in Prompt 45, you only have to update the mapping in one place (narrationContext.ts) and the "voice" of the game updates automatically.
### 5. Deterministic Mapping
One small implementation detail to watch for in narrationContext.ts: ensure the mapping from numbers to labels is **deterministic and tiered**.
For example:
 * **Fatigue 0-2:** "You feel sharp and alert."
 * **Fatigue 3-5:** "A weight sits behind your eyes."
 * **Fatigue 6+:** "Your thoughts move like thick honey."
### Final Verdict
This is the most robust version of the plan yet. It preserves the **"Engine as Source of Truth"** principle while giving the **"Narrator"** the high-quality, filtered data it needs to produce Rothfuss-tier prose.
